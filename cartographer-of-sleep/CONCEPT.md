# The Cartographer of Sleep

## What the game is
You are a cartographer for a small agency that maps the final dreams of the dying, as a form of witness. Your patient is Iris Venn, 81, asleep for nine nights. Each night you are admitted to her dreamscape through a brass door in your office, and you walk through one room of her life, annotating a paper map. Your annotations *confirm* features — unconfirmed features are ghostly, unfixed, uncertain; a confirmed bench becomes a real bench with its grain and its lichen and the memory of her sitting on it. Each night's map must be completed and filed. On the ninth night she does not dream. You sit in your office and type her name on the final form.

## Core idea (one sentence)
Cartography is the last form of love — you cannot save anyone, but you can make sure they were here.

## Two tones that shouldn't go together
Bureaucratic cartographer-precision (grids, scale bars, north arrows, legends, stamps, triplicate forms) against the raw private intimacy of someone else's dying memories (a kitchen in 1958, a miscarriage in 1971, the shoreline where she spread her husband's ashes in 2018).

## Three spaces, named (of seven total)
- **The Swimming Pool** — signature cyan; temporal signature: slow chlorinated breath on a 6-second wave period; underwater filtered cue.
- **The Miscarriage Room** — signature grey-violet; temporal signature: near-silence punctuated by a distant church bell every 23 seconds; no music.
- **The Lighthouse** — signature cold-white with sodium-orange pulse; temporal signature: foghorn every 12s; storm wind on a 9s gust cycle.

(Others: The Office [hub], Summer Kitchen [amber, kettle rhythm], Garden Path [green, cicada pulse], Shoreline [dawn gradient, long breath], Empty Room [ending].)

## Ending
She does not dream; you file the final form by typing her name, and the map of every night you've drawn unfolds on the office wall as a single continuous city of her life, then the lights go out one room at a time.
